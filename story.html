The file / The exhibition century

The shows that made it public.

The Russian avant-garde lived in exhibitions — temporary, argumentative and endlessly re-staged. From a single corner in 1915 to a museum retrospective in 1992, these are the rooms where the new art met its audience.

1915

Petrograd

At '0,10 — The Last Futurist Exhibition', Malevich hangs the Black Square in the corner reserved for an icon. Suprematism is announced in a room, not a manifesto.

1921

Moscow

'5×5=25' gathers Rodchenko, Popova, Stepanova, Exter and Vesnin. Rodchenko shows three monochrome panels and declares easel painting finished.

1922

Berlin

'The First Russian Art Exhibition' at Galerie van Diemen carries the new art abroad; Lissitzky designs the catalogue and the room.

1925

Paris

At the Exposition des Arts Décoratifs, Melnikov's Soviet Pavilion and Rodchenko's Workers' Club put Constructivism in front of an international public.

1932

Leningrad

'Fifteen Years of Artists of the RSFSR' still finds room for the avant-garde before Socialist Realism closes the frame.

1992

New York

'The Great Utopia' at the Guggenheim reassembles the movement for a new century — the shows had become history worth re-staging.

Each show closed; the record did not. Catalogues, checklists and photographs are what let a movement outlive its rooms — and what this paper is built to keep on file.
